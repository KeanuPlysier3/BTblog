Today we sat down for the UX expert, and looked at our UI and current line concept. One of the main conclusions was our queue and mini game still lean too much on text. For families with kids from 6 to 12, many of them beginning readers, that could form a problem. On top of that it is also less engaging.

The queue doesn't have to be a sentence

Right now the phone shows "Er zijn nog 4 groepen voor jullie" at the top of the screen, all the time. The expert's tip: think of a slaapschaap. Children don't count down in numbers but by seeing the color of a nightlight change. They know just by looking if it is time to wake up, or if they have to stay in bed for a second. So preferably the queue becomes visual instead of one boring line that's constantly stuck at the top. 

Guide without words

The second tip was to look at LEGO games, which guide players without any text. Research backs this up. Children remember an action better when they act it out than when they hear it explained (Makri & Fiske, 2023). A visual cue on what to touch helps children with attention difficulties keep up (Bali et al., 2025). So our demo becomes a ghost finger that traces the symbol on the first book. If nobody swipes for a few seconds, it comes back once as a hint. Players choose how many play by tapping hats instead of reading a question.

Make it feel alive

The expert also missed small animations, especially in the mini game. A saved book should feel like a reward: a poof, sparkles, the book flying to the shelf, the score ticking up. Game feel research calls this "juice": generous feedback on every action, which is also how players learn to play (Pichlmair & Johansen, 2021). For feedback, particles work best, and a word like "Poef!" on top makes it even more appealing (Fabre et al., 2024).

What's next

Before we change anything, I'm going to brainstorm on how to show the queue and guide players with less text. Then we'll check the hierarchy, the consistency and the details the design expert pointed out across all screens. In the next test round, we'll see whether the experience is still clear with fewer words.
